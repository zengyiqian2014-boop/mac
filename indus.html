I. The Indus Valley Civilisation

Ancient city planning shows how buildings can stay cooler by working with wind and shade.

Designing With Wind and Shade

The Indus Valley Civilisation planned cities such as Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro with organised streets, drainage and carefully built homes. One useful sustainability lesson is that buildings can be designed to work with the local climate. Streets, courtyards, thick walls and shaded spaces helped people live more comfortably with less need for extra energy.

Sustainability Lesson

The Indus example reminds us that comfort does not always have to come from machines. If a space is planned with airflow, shade and suitable materials, it can stay cooler naturally and use less energy.

Exhibition Idea

A school can create a Cool Corridor model showing shaded walking routes, plants, open airflow and light-coloured surfaces. The model can explain how ancient planning ideas can make a modern school environment more comfortable.

Why It Can Work

The idea can start small. One hot walkway or courtyard can be studied, shaded, planted and compared before and after using simple temperature readings. This makes the idea realistic and easy for pupils to explain during the exhibition.

What We Can Do

Pupils can observe which areas feel hottest, record temperatures, care for plants and explain why natural cooling matters. The key message is that thoughtful design can reduce waste before extra energy is used.

Presentation Line

"The Indus Valley teaches us that good design can make a place cooler before machines are needed."

Why It Is Sustainable

It saves energy, improves comfort and uses greenery to make school spaces healthier.
